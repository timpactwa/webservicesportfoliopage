# Sales and Outreach

## Lead Source

Initial leads will likely come from cold outreach to local businesses in Morris County and nearby areas.

One strong starting source is a local town business directory. If the directory includes business name, email, phone, and website, it can become the first lead dataset.

First source:

```text
https://randolphlocal.com/directory/
```

Important: scraped or directory-provided website data should be double-checked manually. A missing website field may mean the business has no website, but it can also mean the directory is outdated.

First outreach segment:

- Businesses with no website listed in Randolph Local
- Then manually verify with Google search and the business name before outreach
- Only contact businesses that still appear to have no active website or have no clear web presence

Good target categories:

- Landscapers
- Contractors
- Salons and barbershops
- Restaurants and cafes
- Fitness studios
- Local medical and wellness practices
- Dentists
- Real estate agents
- Small retailers
- Professional services

Best early targets are businesses where the website is visibly outdated, slow, broken on mobile, missing clear calls to action, or failing to explain services well.

## Recommended Outreach Strategy

Use a multi-step approach instead of relying only on one channel.

Recommended system:

1. Build a lead list.
2. Pull available business name, email, phone, website, and category from the local business directory.
3. Check whether each website exists and whether the listed URL is current.
4. Review each business website manually.
5. Record a short audit note.
6. Send a personalized email.
7. Follow up once or twice.
8. Call the strongest leads.
9. Book a short consultation call.

This is better than sending generic emails because local business owners get spammed constantly. The outreach has to prove that you actually looked at their business.

Use the embedded Calendly scheduler for booked calls.

## Lead Scraping and Verification

Directory scraping can help create the first outreach list, but it should be treated as lead research, not truth.

Lead verification checklist:

- Does the directory list a website?
- Does the website still load?
- Is it the correct business?
- Is the site mobile-friendly?
- Is there a clear call to action?
- Is there a contact form?
- Is the business active?
- Does Google show a different or newer website?
- Does the business have a Google Business Profile?
- Is the email likely to reach the owner or only a generic inbox?

Priority lead types:

- No website listed and no website found after checking
- Website exists but is broken, slow, outdated, or not mobile-friendly
- Website exists but lacks contact forms, booking, clear services, or service-area pages
- Business has good reviews but weak web presence
- Business looks active and profitable enough to pay

Scraping note:

- Randolph Local is a WordPress/Sabai directory.
- Some directory content appears in the page HTML, but some requests can return a bot-verification page.
- Any scraper should fetch politely, avoid high request rates, and stop if the site serves a verification challenge.
- Do not treat scraped data as complete until it is manually checked.

## Email vs Calling

Use both, but with different purposes.

Email is good for:

- Sending a clear, low-pressure pitch
- Sharing specific observations
- Giving the owner something to review later
- Including a booking link

Calling is good for:

- Getting attention faster
- Reaching owners who do not check email often
- Following up after a personalized email
- Building local trust quickly

Recommended order:

1. Personalized email first.
2. Call 1-2 days later and reference the email.
3. Send a short follow-up email if they do not answer.

## Outreach Email Template

Subject ideas:

```text
Quick idea for [Business Name]'s website
```

```text
Local website help for [Business Name]
```

Email:

```text
Hi [Name],

I came across [Business Name] and noticed [specific observation about their website, Google presence, mobile experience, or missing call to action].

My partner Tim and I run L&T Consulting and Design. We build modern websites and simple automations for local businesses in the Morris County area.

I had one quick idea: [specific improvement]. It could make it easier for customers to understand your services and contact you from their phone.

Would you be open to a free consultation this week? No pressure either way. I can also send over a few notes if that is easier.

Best,
Luke
L&T Consulting and Design
```

## Call Script

Opening:

```text
Hi, is this [Name]? This is Luke with L&T Consulting and Design. I sent over a quick email about [Business Name]'s website and had one specific idea I wanted to run by you. Do you have 30 seconds?
```

If yes:

```text
I noticed [specific issue]. We help local businesses modernize their websites and add simple automations so customers can find information and reach out more easily. I am not calling to pressure you into anything. I wanted to see if improving the website is something you have thought about recently.
```

If interested:

```text
That makes sense. The best next step would be a free consultation where we learn about the business, what you want the site to do, and whether we can help. What does your schedule look like this week?
```

If not interested:

```text
No problem. Would it be helpful if I sent over the quick notes anyway? Even if we never work together, they may give you a few useful ideas.
```

## Lead Tracking

Track every lead, even manually at first.

Useful fields:

- Business name
- Website URL
- Directory website URL if different from verified URL
- Contact name
- Email
- Phone
- Industry
- Town
- Current website quality
- Main issue observed
- Outreach date
- Follow-up date
- Status
- Notes
- Call booked
- Closed or lost reason
- Source directory
- Last verified date

This can start in a spreadsheet. Later it can move into a CRM or automated system.

## Sales Call Goal

The first call is not only to sell. It is to diagnose.

The call should answer:

- What does the business do?
- What problem does the current website have?
- What does the owner want customers to do?
- Is the owner serious enough to pay?
- Is there enough information to quote?
- Would an automation actually help?

## Offer on the Call

If the client is a fit, explain the process simply:

```text
After this call, we send a short project summary, a Stripe payment link for the deposit, and a Google Forms intake form. Once the deposit and form are complete, we start the build.
```

This makes the business feel organized without needing a full automated client portal on day one.
