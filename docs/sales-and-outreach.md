# Sales and Outreach

This is the working playbook for finding local clients and the paste-ready copy kit for every message we send. Update copy here first, then use it.

## How a lead becomes a client (the real flow)

There is no Calendly, no Stripe payment link, and no Google Forms. The flow is simple and manual on purpose:

1. A prospect finds us, or we reach out, and they submit the on-site "Request a Call" form at `/contact`. The free homepage mockup request lives at `/contact?intent=mockup`. Both send an email to `support@renderlocalwebsites.com`.
2. One of us replies the same day to set up a short call. We schedule by email, not a booking widget.
3. On the call we scope what they need. Then we show a first design direction before any deposit is taken. If it is not right, they walk away and owe nothing.
4. If they approve the direction, we send a short project summary and invoice the deposit directly. The paid deposit starts the build.
5. Most sites launch in 2 to 4 weeks after we have their content. The optional Care Plan keeps it maintained after launch, month to month, no contract.

Everything below serves that flow: fill the top with good leads, and use the copy kit to move each one to the next step without sounding like a template.

## Lead source

Early leads come from cold outreach to local businesses in Morris County and nearby towns.

One strong starting source is a local business directory. If it lists business name, email, phone, and website, it can become the first lead dataset.

First source:

```text
https://randolphlocal.com/directory/
```

Important: scraped or directory-provided website data has to be double-checked by hand. A missing website field may mean the business has no site, or it may just mean the directory is out of date.

First outreach segment:

- Businesses with no website listed in Randolph Local
- Verify manually with a Google search and the business name before any outreach
- Only contact businesses that still appear to have no active site or no clear web presence

Good target categories:

- Landscapers
- Contractors
- Salons and barbershops
- Restaurants and cafes
- Fitness studios
- Local medical and wellness practices
- Dentists
- Real estate agents
- Small retailers
- Professional services

Best early targets are businesses whose site is visibly outdated, slow, broken on mobile, missing a clear call to action, or failing to explain their services.

## Lead scraping and verification

Directory scraping helps build the first outreach list, but treat it as lead research, not truth.

Lead verification checklist:

- Does the directory list a website?
- Does the website still load?
- Is it the correct business?
- Is the site mobile-friendly?
- Is there a clear call to action?
- Is there a contact form?
- Is the business active?
- Does Google show a different or newer website?
- Does the business have a Google Business Profile?
- Is the email likely to reach the owner or only a generic inbox?

Priority lead types:

- No website listed and none found after checking
- Website exists but is broken, slow, outdated, or not mobile-friendly
- Website exists but lacks contact forms, booking, clear services, or service-area pages
- Business has good reviews but a weak web presence
- Business looks active and profitable enough to pay

Scraping note:

- Randolph Local is a WordPress and Sabai directory.
- Some content appears in the page HTML, but some requests return a bot-verification page.
- Fetch politely, keep request rates low, and stop if the site serves a verification challenge.
- Do not treat scraped data as complete until it is checked by hand.

## Email versus calling

Use both, with different jobs.

Email is good for:

- Sending a clear, low-pressure pitch
- Sharing a specific observation
- Giving the owner something to review on their own time
- Sending a link to a live demo or the request-a-call form

Calling is good for:

- Getting attention faster
- Reaching owners who do not check email often
- Following up after a personalized email
- Building local trust quickly

Recommended order:

1. Personalized email first.
2. Call 1 to 2 days later and reference the email.
3. Send a short follow-up email if they do not answer.

## Call script

Opening:

```text
Hi, is this [Name]? This is Tim with Render. I sent over a quick email about [Business Name]'s website and had one specific idea I wanted to run by you. Do you have 30 seconds?
```

If yes:

```text
I noticed [specific issue]. We help local businesses modernize their websites and add simple automations so customers can find them and reach out more easily. I am not calling to pressure you into anything. I just wanted to see if the website is something you have thought about recently.
```

If interested:

```text
That makes sense. The best next step is a quick call where we learn about the business, what you want the site to do, and whether we can help. You see a first design direction before any deposit, so there is no risk in looking. What does your week look like?
```

If not interested:

```text
No problem. Want me to send over the quick notes anyway? Even if we never work together, they may give you a few useful ideas.
```

## The sales call

The first call is not only to sell. It is to diagnose.

The call should answer:

- What does the business do?
- What problem does the current website have?
- What does the owner want customers to do?
- Is the owner serious enough to pay?
- Is there enough information to quote?
- Would an automation actually help?

If they are a fit, explain what happens next in plain terms:

```text
After this call, I show you a first design direction. If it is right, I send a short project summary and an invoice for the deposit. Once the deposit is in, we start the build, and most sites launch in 2 to 4 weeks after we have your content.
```

That keeps the process organized without needing a client portal on day one. No booking widget, no payment link to set up, just a summary, a direct invoice, and a start date.

Useful fields:

- Business name
- Website URL
- Directory website URL if different from the verified URL
- Contact name
- Email
- Phone
- Industry
- Town
- Current website quality
- Main issue observed
- Outreach date
- Follow-up date

Start in a spreadsheet. It can move into a CRM later.

# Copy kit

Paste-ready messages for every step. Rules that apply to all of them:

- Write like a real person, first person, from Tim. No corporate speak. No "solutions," "elevate," or "unlock."
- Keep it a 20-second read. If it scrolls on a phone, cut it.
- No em dashes. No exclamation marks. Use a period or restructure.
- Swap the sentences, not just the brackets. Same skeleton, fresh wording per prospect.
- Personalize the `[bracketed]` slots with one real detail that proves a human looked.
- Never link a demo that is not actually built and live. If there is no demo yet, offer to build the free mockup instead.
- Append the compliance footer below to every cold email before sending.

Compliance footer (append to every cold outreach email):

```text
Render, [your mailing address, town, NJ ZIP]. Not interested? Just reply and I will take you off my list for good.
```

## 1. Cold email (first touch)

Subject line options, pick one and rotate across the list:

```text
a homepage idea for [Business Name]
```

```text
built [Business Name] a sample site
```

```text
quick note on [Business Name]'s website
```

Body (single CTA, a reply or a look at the link, never a 45-minute meeting):

```text
Hi [First Name],

I came across [Business Name] while looking at [town] [category] and noticed [one real detail, like your reviews are great but the site is slow on my phone, or there is no way to book online].

My co-founder Luke and I run Render, a small web studio here in Morris County. I put together a sample homepage for [Business Name] so you can see what a faster, modern version could look like, live and clickable, not a slideshow: [demo link]

Nothing owed, and no call needed to look. If you want changes, or you would rather I never email again, just say so.

Worth a look?

Tim
Render, renderlocalwebsites.com
```

Note: this reads best when the demo link is a homepage you actually built for them, which is our demo-first motion. If you have not built one yet, change the hook to an offer: "I can put together a free sample homepage for [Business Name] this week if you want to see it," and point them at `renderlocalwebsites.com/contact?intent=mockup`.

## 2. Cold follow-ups

Maximum two, then stop. Each one adds something new, never "just bumping this."

Follow-up 1, send 3 to 4 days after the first email (under 60 words):

```text
Subject: still there if it is useful

Hi [First Name], following up on the sample homepage I built for [Business Name]: [demo link]. I tightened the [section, like the contact area] so it is easier to reach you from a phone. If it is close, I can match your colors and photos. If not, no worries. Want me to send it over?

Tim
```

Follow-up 2, send 7 to 10 days after the first email (breakup, under 50 words):

```text
Subject: last note from me

Hi [First Name], I will stop here so I am not crowding your inbox. Your sample site stays up a couple weeks if you want it: [demo link]. If a modern site with local SEO is ever worth a look, just reply. Wishing [Business Name] a strong season.

Tim
```

## 3. Post-inquiry sequence (they submitted the form)

They filled out the request-a-call or mockup form, so the email landed at `support@renderlocalwebsites.com`. Reply fast. Speed matters more than polish here.

Email A, reply the same day, ideally within the hour:

```text
Subject: got your note, [First Name]

Hi [First Name], thanks for reaching out through the site. I saw your request come in for [Business Name].

One quick question so our call is useful: what is the main thing you want the site to do, like get more calls, take bookings, or just look current?

I am around most mornings this week and [today or tomorrow] afternoon. Reply with a couple of times that suit you and I will confirm one.

Talk soon,
Tim
```

Email B, send 2 days later if they have not answered:

```text
Subject: still happy to help, [First Name]

Hi [First Name], circling back on your note about [Business Name]. No rush on my end, I know things get busy.

If it is easier, send me the best time to reach you and the number to use, and I will call you. Or if now is not the moment, just say so and I will check back down the road.

Tim
```

## 4. No-show sequence (booked call, did not answer)

We had a time set and they did not pick up. Keep it warm and low-guilt.

Email A, same day:

```text
Subject: sorry we missed each other

Hi [First Name], looks like the timing did not work out for our call today. That happens, no worries at all.

Want to try again? Send me a couple of times that work this week and I will lock one in. If a call feels like too much, I am glad to just send a few notes on [Business Name]'s site instead.

Tim
```

Email B, send 3 days later:

```text
Subject: want me to just send notes?

Hi [First Name], no worries about the missed call, I do not want to chase you.

If you still want a fresh site, or a quick read on what is holding [Business Name]'s current one back, reply here and I will send a short list, no call needed. If the timing is off, I will check back another time.

Tim
```

## 5. Post-launch sequence (the site is live)

Handoff email, send the day the site goes live:

```text
Subject: [Business Name] is live

Hi [First Name], your new site is live: [site URL]. It is fully yours, and I am glad it is out in the world.

A few things to know:
- It is built for speed and set up for local search, so [town] customers can find you.
- Reach me any time something needs a change.
- Your Care Plan is optional and month to month, no contract. It covers hosting support, small content updates, and fixes when you need them. Start it, skip it, or add it later, your call.

Take a look and tell me if anything needs a tweak. I want it right.

Tim
```

Check-in, send 14 days after launch (asks for the review and a referral):

```text
Subject: how is the new site treating you?

Hi [First Name], your site has been live two weeks now. How is it feeling? If anything looks off or you want a small change, send it over and I will handle it.

Two small asks if you are happy with it:
- A quick Google review would mean a lot for a new studio like ours: [Google review link].
- If you know another [town] owner who needs a better site, send them my way and I will take good care of them.

Either way, thank you for being one of our first clients.

Tim
```

Short enough to send as a text, complete enough to send as an email. Send it once they have told you they are happy.

```text

First, would you leave us a Google review? Here is the direct link: [Google review link]. As one of our first clients, your words carry real weight.
```

## 8. Google Business Profile registration checklist

GBP is free, it is the top surface for "web designer near me" searches, and Google's AI answers pull from it. Set it up completely before any other directory work.

Steps:

1. Create or claim the profile at google.com/business under a Google account we will keep long term (the shared Render account, not a personal one).
2. Business name: enter it exactly as it reads on the site footer, "Render". Do not add keywords or a town to the name.
3. Category: choose "Website designer" as the primary category. Add "Marketing consultant" or "Internet marketing service" as secondary categories if the options fit.
4. This is a service-area business, not a storefront. When asked, hide the street address and set a service area instead. List Morris County, NJ plus Randolph, Denville, Morristown, Parsippany, and Madison, matching the towns named in the site footer.
5. Verify the profile (Google will send a code by phone, email, or postcard). Nothing shows publicly until verification is done.
6. Add the website: `renderlocalwebsites.com`. Add all three demo links under products or services if the format allows.
7. Write the description: paste section 6 above.
8. Add photos: the founder headshots of Tim and Luke used on the About page, plus clean screenshots of the three demo sites.
9. Turn on messaging only if we can answer it same day. If not, leave it off so we never look unresponsive.
10. Prepare the review link now so it is ready the moment client number one is happy (section 7 uses it).

NAP consistency (name, address, phone must match the site footer and every other listing exactly):

- Name: Render. Same spelling and capitalization everywhere.
- Address: no public street address. We are a service-area business, so the location stays "Morris County, NJ" to match the footer. Do not invent a street address to fill the field.
- Phone: use one real number we can actually answer, and use that same number on every listing from here on. The footer currently lists no phone, so decide on the number first, then add it to the footer and GBP together so they match.
- Website: `renderlocalwebsites.com`.
- Email: `support@renderlocalwebsites.com` (kept consistent even though GBP does not display it publicly).

Any mismatch across listings weakens local ranking, so pick the exact name, number, and service area once and reuse them everywhere.

## What we can and cannot claim

Honesty is the whole pitch. Do not undercut it in outreach.

- The three demo sites (Fuego Street Tacos, Hartline Builders, Marlowe Dental Studio) are demo templates we designed and built to show what we would do for a business like the prospect's. They are never described as real businesses or paying clients.
- Hope Tree Family Services is a real consulting engagement (nonprofit strategy work), not a website client. Reference it as consulting proof only. Never present it as a website build or imply it is a web client.
- No fabricated clients, results, reviews, or numbers. We do not claim a testimonial, a metric, or a client win until it is real. The site's testimonials stay empty until a paying client gives us a quote, and outreach follows the same rule.
- Founding client pricing and "one of our first clients" are true today, so they are fair to say. When the portfolio fills out and prices rise, update the copy.
