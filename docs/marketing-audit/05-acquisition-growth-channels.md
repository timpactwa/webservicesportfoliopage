## 2. Prioritized channel stack

Ranked by expected ROI for a 2-person, pre-revenue-proof local agency, using the ORB lens (owned/rented/borrowed channels compound into owned relationships; paid is a volume amplifier, not a starting point).

**P0 - Cold email + phone (owned list, borrowed attention)**
This is already the core motion and it should stay the top investment. Local SMB cold outreach against a verified list is the only channel where Render controls both the message and the target, and it's the one channel already producing a client. Cost is time, not dollars. At 2 people covering ~25-40 businesses/week, this can realistically produce 1-2 booked calls/week once the sequence and subject lines are tightened (see section 3). Nothing else in this stack works without this list-building muscle already in place, so double down before adding channels.

**P0 - Referrals (owned relationship, zero CAC)**
For a local service business, referrals convert at the highest rate and cost the least, because trust transfers from a known person. Render only has ~1 client so far, which means the referral program can't run at volume yet, but the ask needs to be built into the process now so it fires the moment client #1 is happy. This is the single highest-leverage channel once there are 3-5 happy clients (see section 4) - higher leverage than paid ads will ever be at this business size.

**P0 - Google Business Profile (rented, but free and highest local buyer-intent)**
GBP is effectively a directory + review engine + local pack ranking system in one, and it's free. Every Morris County business owner searching "web designer near me" sees the local pack before organic results. This should be live and optimized before any other directory work.

**P1 - Directory & local listings (rented, compounding backlinks + AI citation surface)**
Bing Places, Chamber directory, Clutch, NJ/Morris County business directories. Lower urgency than GBP but each is a one-time setup with a long payoff tail (backlinks, local SEO, and increasingly, LLM citation surface per the directory-submissions skill's GEO section). Batch this in one afternoon.

**P1 - Co-marketing / local partnerships (borrowed audience)**
Photographers, printers, marketing consultants who don't build websites, and the Chamber are Render's highest-fit partners because they share Render's audience (local business owners) without competing for the same budget. Requires outreach effort but zero cash spend. Realistic to land 2-3 active partners in the first 90 days.

**P1 - Sales enablement collateral (supports every other channel)**
Not a channel by itself, but the one-pager, proposal template, and case-study template are what make cold email, referrals, and co-marketing actually convert once a prospect is warm. This should be built in parallel with the above, not after.

**P2 - Social + short-form video (rented, builds proof + SEO/GEO surface over time)**
Worth doing at low, consistent effort (1-2 posts/week) because "teardown" video content doubles as prospecting ammunition (personalization fodder for cold email) and as public proof once shared. Not a primary lead source at this follower count; treat as a compounding side asset, not a growth channel to optimize weekly.

**P2 - Lifecycle/nurture email (owned, protects pipeline that already exists)**
Render has no post-inquiry nurture today - Calendly no-shows and non-responders currently fall into a void. This is cheap to build and directly plugs a leak in the existing pipeline, so it's higher priority than any channel that requires net-new audience building.

**P3 - SMS (owned, client-ops tool not acquisition)**
Scoped to project comms and review requests only. Not a lead-gen channel for this business model - see section 11.

**P3 (not now) - Paid ads (rented, cash-for-speed)**
Explicitly deferred per the brief. No proof asset (testimonial) yet, budget is better spent as time on P0 channels, and Google/Meta ad accounts punish landing pages and offers that haven't been battle-tested organically first. Trigger conditions defined in section 10.

## 3. Cold email upgrade (cold-email + prospecting)

### Critique of the current demo-first motion

The brief's current sequence: verify site quality → personalized cold email referencing a real observation → phone follow-up 1-2 days later → free 45-min Calendly consult. This is fundamentally sound (personalization + phone follow-up is exactly what the cold-email skill's benchmarks reward), but three things are working against it:

1. **The first ask is too heavy.** A 45-minute Calendly consult is a big first commitment for someone who just opened a cold email. The skill's data is explicit: interest-based CTAs ("worth a look?") outperform meeting requests in the first touch. Render should have a lighter-weight yes available - "want me to send it over?" - before "book 45 minutes with two strangers."
2. **The demo-first hook is good but underused as the subject line driver.** "We built you a free sample homepage" is a strong, concrete offer - the skill calls this exact pattern out as one of the best cold-email angles that exists (unsolicited proof beats claims). But if the subject line doesn't hint at this, opens will lag. Subject lines should telegraph "something specific about you" or "something already made for you," not sound like generic outreach.
3. **No visible follow-up variation.** The brief only describes cadence (3-7-7, ~93% of replies captured), not angle rotation. Per the skill, "just checking in" gives zero reason to reply - each follow-up needs a new angle or it burns the list.

### Subject-line bank (all under 6 words, lowercase, boring-looking per skill guidance - avoid punctuation/emoji/business name)

1. quick look at your site
2. built you something
3. mockup for [business name]
4. homepage idea for you
5. noticed this on your site
6. free mockup, no pitch
7. 10 min could help
8. saw your site today

Rotate these; never reuse the same one twice against the same list segment (deliverability + it reads as spam if repeated).

### Improved cold email template (free-mockup hook, demo-first)

```
Subject: mockup for [Business Name]

Hi [First Name],

I was looking at [Business Name]'s site earlier and noticed [specific
observation - slow load, no mobile menu, outdated photos, no pricing,
etc.]. Given how [specific detail: busy street, high Google reviews,
niche] you clearly are, that's probably costing you calls.

So I built a sample homepage for [Business Name] to show what a faster,
modern version could look like. It's live, not a mockup image - you can
click through it like a customer would: [demo link]

No pitch attached. If it's useful, happy to walk you through what's
different. If not, no worries either way.

Worth a look?

Tim
Render - renderlocalwebsites.com
```

Why this beats the likely current draft: leads with their world (specific observation), the ask is "worth a look" not "book a call," and the free mockup is already attached/linked rather than promised - proof over claim, which the skill flags as the single strongest lever in cold email.

### 3-7-7 follow-up sequence copy

**Touch 1 - Day 0 (email above)**

**Touch 2 - Day 3 (new angle: make it concrete, remove friction)**
```
Subject: still there if you want it

Hey [First Name], following up on the sample homepage I built for
[Business Name] - [demo link]. Totally fine if now's not the time.
If you want changes or just want to see how it'd work on mobile,
reply here and I'll send it over.
```

**Touch 3 - Day 10 (proof/reframe angle, not "checking in")**
```
Subject: 10 min could help

[First Name] - most owners I talk to in Morris County didn't realize
their site wasn't showing up when people searched "[category] near
[town]" until we checked. Happy to run that same check on
[Business Name] free, no strings. Want me to send what I find?
```

**Touch 4 - Day 17 (phone-first pivot + soft breakup)**
```
Subject: last note from me

[First Name], I'll stop emailing after this one. If a faster, modern
site (with local SEO built in) is worth 10 minutes sometime, call/text
me at [number] or grab a time here: [Calendly link]. Either way, wishing
[Business Name] a good [season/local event reference].

Tim
```

Ties to the brief's benchmarks: 7% SMB reply rate baseline, 3-7-7 cadence capturing ~93% of eventual replies, Tue-Thu 8-11am sends, subject <6 words - all preserved, with the angle-rotation and lighter-CTA fixes layered in per the cold-email skill.

**P0**: rewrite the live template and subject bank this week - zero cost, direct lift on the highest-priority channel.
**P1**: add a "send the demo, no call required" reply path so prospects self-serve to a lighter yes.

## 4. Referrals - the local referral program

This is the highest-leverage channel available to Render and currently doesn't exist as a formal program. Per the referrals skill: referred customers convert at higher rates, churn less, and refer again at 2-3x the base rate - and for a local agency, referral trust transfers directly from one small-business owner to another in the same community, which cold email can never fully replicate.

### Structure: single-sided → double-sided as volume grows

**Phase 1 (now, 1-3 clients): simple thank-you ask, no formal incentive yet.** Formal cash incentives look premature with only 1 client and could read as buying reviews. Ask for the referral as a favor, backed by a small thank-you gesture.

**Phase 2 (once 3+ happy clients exist): double-sided reward.**
- Referring client gets: **1 month off their maintenance plan** (worth $50-125 depending on tier) or a **$100 credit toward future work** (logo refresh, extra page, etc.)
- Referred business gets: **$100 off their first invoice** or a **free add-on** (e.g., the AI intake automation normally $250+, at no charge)
- Double-sided beats single-sided per the skill's data - it reframes the ask as "here's something good for both of you" instead of "do me a favor."

### Timing: the ask happens at peak satisfaction, not randomly

Per the skill's "trigger moment" framework, the single best moment is **right after the "aha" moment** - for a web client, that's launch day, when the new site goes live and they can see/show it off, not weeks later.

**Referral ask script (send at launch, via email + verbally on the handoff call):**
```
Subject: your site is live! one favor

[First Name] - [Business Name] is officially live: [URL]. Congrats,
it looks great.

One favor if you don't mind: if you know another local business owner
who's been putting off their website (or hates their current one), send
them my way. I'll take care of them the same way I took care of you,
and I'll knock $100 off your next invoice or a month off your plan for
every business that becomes a client.

No pressure either way, just wanted to ask while it's fresh.

Tim
```

**Second touch, 30-60 days post-launch (in the lifecycle sequence, section 7):** a lighter reminder tied to a result ("your site's been live a month, here's what's changed") with the referral ask restated in one line.

### Partner referrals (non-competing local vendors)

Per the co-marketing skill's partner-scoring framework (audience fit, brand alignment, reciprocity, ease of execution), the strongest local partner types are:

1. **Web designers/freelancers who don't do full custom builds** (Wix/Squarespace DIYers, print/graphic designers who get asked "can you also build my site") - refer overflow work both directions.
2. **Local photographers** - every new site needs photos; every photography client eventually needs a site. Natural two-way referral.
3. **Printers/sign shops** - same logic; business owners getting signage made are mid-rebrand and need a matching website.
4. **Local marketing/social-media consultants who don't build sites** - they need a web partner to refer clients to, Render needs ongoing-marketing partners to refer clients to.
5. **Accountants/bookkeepers serving small businesses** - high trust, sees every local business that's growing or rebranding.

**Approach:** 15-minute coffee or call, not a cold pitch. Offer a simple 10% referral fee or reciprocal referral commitment (no cash needed either direction, just an agreement to send business back). Target 3-5 of these relationships in the first 90 days - this overlaps with section 6's Chamber/community targets.

**P0**: build the launch-day referral ask into the standard client handoff (it currently isn't part of the process - this is a process gap, not just a marketing gap).
**P1**: formalize the double-sided incentive once client #3 closes.
**P1**: start partner conversations with 1 photographer and 1 non-competing designer this month.

## 5. Directory & local presence (directory-submissions)

The directory-submissions skill is built for SaaS/software launches (Product Hunt, G2, AI directories); most of its tier system doesn't apply. The relevant piece is **Tier 9 (local business directories)** plus the core principle that carries over regardless of vertical: **destination pages before directories, and never duplicate the same description everywhere.**

Render's "destination" isn't a template gallery, it's the **Work page and the three live demo sites** - those need to be in good shape (they already are, per the brief) before pushing hard on directory volume, since that's where directory traffic lands.

### Priority order

**P0 - Google Business Profile.** Do this first and do it completely: category (Website Designer + Marketing Consultant), service area (Morris County towns named explicitly), all three demo links in the "products/services" section if the format allows, business description written fresh (not copy-pasted from the site), photos of Tim and Luke (named-founder trust signal doubles here), and the review request flow set up now so it's ready the moment client #1 will leave one. GBP is also the #1 surface local "near me" search and Google's AI Overviews pull from.

**P0 - Bing Places.** Free, five minutes, meaningfully feeds Bing/Copilot local results which a nontrivial share of older local business owners still use.

**P1 - Morris County / NJ local directories.** Morris County Chamber of Commerce member directory (bundle with the Chamber membership in section 6 - same action, two payoffs), NJ.com local business listings if available, PatchLocal (Morris County has active Patch sites per town - Randolph, Morristown, etc.), any township-run "shop local" directory (many NJ towns maintain one).

**P1 - Clutch and Clutch-adjacent agency directories.** Clutch requires client reviews to have real weight (same 10-in-30 review-drive logic as G2, from the directory-submissions skill) - worth claiming the profile now, but don't expect traffic until 2-3 client reviews exist. Also list on **DesignRush** and **The Manifest** (Clutch's sister sites, same review requirement).

**P2 - Niche web-design directories.** These carry less local buyer intent than Chamber/Clutch and are lower priority; only pursue after P0/P1 are done. Skip generic "best of" SaaS directories entirely (TAAFT, Futurepedia, etc.) - wrong audience, per the skill's own positioning-by-surface table, none of those surfaces reach Morris County small-business owners.

**Positioning discipline:** per the skill's Rule 3, write a distinct one-paragraph description for GBP (outcome-led: "modern websites for Morris County businesses"), Chamber (community-led: "founder-led studio based in Morris County"), and Clutch (agency-comparison-led: "founder-led alternative to markup-heavy agencies"). Do not paste the same paragraph into all three.

**P2 - Reviews.** Apply the skill's 10-in-30 logic to GBP directly once there are 3+ clients: personal ask, direct review link (not a form), one follow-up only, small thank-you if appropriate. Zero reviews today is the single biggest local-trust gap alongside the missing case study (per the brief's known-gaps section) - this is the fix.

## 7. Lifecycle email (emails skill)

Render currently has **no lifecycle email at all** between "Calendly call happens" and "project starts," and nothing after launch except presumably ad hoc communication. This is a leak in an otherwise sound pipeline - cheap to fix, no new audience needed.

### Post-inquiry sequence (between call and close)

Trigger: consult call completed, proposal/Stripe deposit link sent but not yet paid.

1. **Same day - recap + next step.** "Great talking today. Here's what we discussed: [scope]. Next step: [Stripe link] to lock in your spot, then the intake form takes 10 minutes." One CTA.
2. **Day 3 - remove friction, answer likely objection.** Short note anticipating the top objection (usually price or timeline): "Most people ask [X] - here's the answer: [X]." Include one proof point (a live demo link).
3. **Day 7 - social proof / urgency (real, not fake).** "Founding client pricing is limited while we build out the portfolio" (true, per the brief's pricing strategy) - this is honest scarcity, not manufactured.
4. **Day 14 - direct ask / soft close.** "Still interested in moving forward? Happy to hop on a quick call if anything's unclear." If no response, move to win-back (below).

### No-show win-back (Calendly no-shows)

1. **Same day (2 hours after missed slot):** "Looks like we missed each other - no worries, want to grab another time?" + rebooking link.
2. **Day 3:** one value-add (the free-mockup offer if not already sent) + rebooking link.
3. **Day 10:** final light touch, then move to long-term nurture (quarterly check-in) rather than continued follow-up.

### Post-client onboarding (after Stripe deposit, before launch)

1. **Immediate:** welcome + what happens next + intake form link + realistic timeline expectations.
2. **Mid-build check-in:** progress update with a preview link if available - reduces anxiety, keeps momentum, and reduces mid-project scope-creep requests by showing progress proactively.
3. **Pre-launch:** what to expect at launch, DNS/domain steps if applicable.

### Post-launch nurture (feeds the referral ask in section 4)

1. **Launch day:** the referral ask email (section 4).
2. **30 days:** "How's the new site working for you?" + review ask (ties to section 5's GBP review drive) + soft referral reminder.
3. **Quarterly:** light value-add (a quick SEO or performance note, "here's how your site's doing") - keeps Render top of mind for the maintenance relationship and future upsell (AI automation add-on).

**P0**: build the post-inquiry sequence (4 emails) and the no-show win-back (3 emails) - these directly protect deals already in the pipeline today.
**P1**: build the post-launch 30-day and quarterly touch - feeds referrals and reviews.

## 8. Sales enablement collateral kit

Per the sales-enablement skill, reps (here, Tim and Luke) only use collateral they trust and that matches their voice - so all of the below should be built once, kept to one page where specified, and reused verbatim rather than improvised per call.

Per the skill's 10-12 slide framework, compressed to what a 2-person local shop actually needs (5-6 slides, no more):

2. What Render does (2 services, one sentence each)

5. **Pull quote**: one sentence, verbatim from the client.
6. **Tags**: industry, use case - so it's reusable in cold email personalization ("we did something similar for a [category] business in [town]").

This case study, once filled, becomes the single highest-impact upgrade to every other channel in this report - it directly plugs the brief's #1 flagged gap (no real proof yet) and should be built the moment client #1 is willing to give a quote.

## 9. Social + video + image (right-sized)

### What a 2-person shop should actually post

Per the social skill's content-pillar framework, scaled down hard: **1-2 posts/week, one platform primary (LinkedIn, since B2B/local-owner audience skews there per the skill's platform table), not a daily content operation.** Don't spread across Instagram/TikTok/LinkedIn simultaneously at this team size - pick LinkedIn as primary, treat Instagram as a lightweight visual home for the teardown clips.

**3 content pillars, kept tight:**
1. **Teardowns/before-after (60%)** - the highest-effort-to-value content type for this business; doubles as prospecting material.
2. **Build-in-public / behind the scenes (25%)** - Tim building a site, Luke doing local research; cheap, authentic, low production.
3. **Local proof / client spotlight (15%)** - once case studies exist; until then, demo spotlights.

### Short-form video ideas (realistic effort)

Per the video skill, this doesn't need AI avatars or programmatic tooling - screen recording + a phone camera is enough at this stage.

1. **"I redesigned a local business's site in 60 seconds"** - timelapse screen recording of Tim building a section of a demo site, sped up, captioned. Cheap (just screen record + CapCut), high shareability, doubles as a skill demonstration for prospects.
2. **Before/after site teardown** - 30-45 sec, split-screen old local business site vs. Render's rebuild concept (use one of the three demo sites as the "after"). Hook: "This [category] business is losing customers because of their website" (problem-agitate-solve structure from the ads skill, works for organic too).
3. **"3 things wrong with your website"** - quick tactical tip format, general (not calling out a specific business by name) so it's shareable and non-confrontational, positions Tim/Luke as the experts.
4. **Founder POV / why we started this** - one authentic story post; per the social skill's data, personal-story content gets 1.5-2x average engagement, and this also does double duty softening the "student-run" perception risk flagged in the brief by making the founder story a source of trust rather than something to downplay.

Keep every video captioned (most social video is watched without sound) and under 60 seconds.

## 10. Paid ads readiness (NOT now)

Per the brief, this is explicitly deferred, and the ads skill's own guidance backs that: launching paid without proven organic conversion and without a proof asset (testimonial/case study) wastes spend, because a cold ad audience converts worse than a warm cold-email lead and the landing page hasn't been battle-tested.

### Trigger to start

Start paid only when **all** of these are true:
1. At least 1 real client case study/testimonial is live on the site (fixes the credibility gap the ad would otherwise expose).
2. GBP has 5+ reviews (an ad clicking through to a 0-review GBP looks worse than no ad).
3. Cold email + referral pipeline is running consistently enough that Render can absorb additional lead volume without missing follow-up SLAs (no point buying leads Render can't call back within the day).
4. There's real budget slack beyond founder time - treat this as a $300-500/mo test budget floor, not $50.

### First campaign: Google Local Services Ads (LSA), not standard Search

For a local service business, **Google Local Services Ads** (the "Google Guaranteed" pay-per-lead local ads, not standard Search PPC) is the better first move per the ads skill's platform-selection table - it's pay-per-lead not pay-per-click, requires a background check/license verification (builds trust automatically), and shows at the very top of local search above even organic and standard ads. Standard Google Search on "web designer near me" / "website design Morris County NJ" is the natural P2 follow-up once LSA is running, using tight geo-targeting (Morris County towns only) and exact local-intent keywords, not broad "web design" terms nationally.

### 3 sample ad-creative concepts (for when the trigger hits)

Per the ad-creative skill's angle framework:

1. **Outcome angle**: Headline "Modern Website, Live in Weeks" / Description "Fixed pricing. No account managers. ## 11. SMS - brief

Per the sms skill, SMS earns the right to interrupt because of opt-in, and its core strength (immediacy) matters for transactional and time-sensitive messages, not cold acquisition. For Render:

- **Not a lead-gen channel.** Cold-texting local businesses is both a worse conversion motion than cold email for a B2B local-service pitch and a TCPA compliance risk not worth taking on at this stage (A2P 10DLC registration, consent records, etc. - real overhead for a 2-person shop with no current SMS infrastructure).
- **Right use, once clients exist:** project-status texts during the build (mirrors the "post-purchase" pattern from the skill) and **review request texts** post-launch, which convert far better via text than email (higher open rate, immediate context). This should piggyback on whatever tool sends the GBP review ask in section 5 - a simple "Hey, here's the link to leave a quick review: [link]" text, sent once, with clear opt-out.
- **P2, not P0/P1.** Build this only after the email lifecycle sequences (section 7) are live; it's a marginal lift on review conversion, not a growth channel.

## 12. RevOps - minimal setup

Render already runs a private lead pipeline tool (per the render-ops context) - this section adapts the revops skill's B2B framework down to what that tool actually needs, not a new CRM.

### Pipeline stages (adapted from the skill's lifecycle table)

| Stage | Entry criteria | Exit criteria | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sourced** | Business added from verified local lead list | Site quality-checked, contact info confirmed | Tim/Luke |
| **Contacted** | First cold email sent | Reply received OR 3-7-7 sequence exhausted | Tim/Luke |
| **Engaged** | Prospect replied or answered a call | Call booked | Tim/Luke |
| **Call Booked** | Calendly confirmed | Call completed | Tim/Luke |
| **Proposal Sent** | Call done, Stripe link + intake sent | Deposit paid | Tim/Luke |
| **Client - In Build** | Deposit paid, intake received | Site launched | Tim |
| **Client - Live** | Site launched | Ongoing (maintenance plan) or churned | Tim/Luke |
| **Referral Source** | Live client, 30+ days, satisfied | Active in referral ask sequence | Luke |

### Minimal required fields per lead

- Business name, category, town, source (which list/method), contact name + verified email/phone, date first contacted, current stage, last touch date, next scheduled touch, objection/reason if lost.

### Lead scoring (simplified - no need for a points model at this volume)

Use the prospecting skill's Hot/Warm/Cold labels directly instead of building a numeric model (a numeric model is overkill below ~50 leads/month):
- **Hot**: outdated/broken site + active local reviews (proves the business is alive and cares about reputation) + a personal email/phone verified.
- **Warm**: decent site but slow/not mobile-optimized, or no site at all but clearly active business.
- **Cold**: site is fine, low buying signal, or contact unverified.

### Follow-up automation (minimum viable, no new tooling required)

- A simple recurring calendar/task reminder tied to the 3-7-7 cadence per lead (day 0, 3, 10, 17) is sufficient at this volume - a full marketing-automation platform is not justified yet for 2 people and a manageable weekly lead count.
- **Speed-to-lead discipline**: per the revops skill, contacting a warm reply within hours dramatically outperforms next-day follow-up - this is a process rule to enforce (whoever sees the reply first responds within the day), not a tooling gap.

### Weekly review (5 minutes, not a dashboard build)

Track weekly: leads sourced, cold emails sent, replies, calls booked, calls held, proposals sent, deposits paid. This funnel view is enough to spot where the pipeline is leaking (e.g., high sends but low replies = fix the email; high replies but low bookings = fix the ask) without needing a BI tool.

**P0**: add the "Referral Source" stage and the required fields above to the existing pipeline tool now - near-zero cost, plugs the tracking gap for section 4's program.
**P1**: adopt the Hot/Warm/Cold labels for list scoring.
**P2**: formal weekly funnel review cadence once volume justifies it (past ~10 leads/week).

## Summary (12 lines)

1. Cold email + referrals + Google Business Profile are the three P0 channels; paid ads are explicitly deferred until a testimonial exists.

9. Sales enablement priority is the after-the-call package and a ready-to-fill case-study template, built before client #1 gives a quote.

11. SMS and paid ads are correctly out of scope for now; SMS supports reviews and project comms, paid ads need a testimonial and consistent follow-up capacity first.
12. RevOps needs only new pipeline stages and fields inside the existing private tool, not a new CRM.
