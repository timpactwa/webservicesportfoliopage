## 2. Positioning statement & messaging hierarchy

### Current problem
The site's implicit positioning is "a founder-led studio that builds websites." That's a company description, not a reason to buy. The brand doc is explicit that outcome should lead and "founder-led" should be supporting proof, but the homepage H1 currently leads with the studio ("We Build the Web for Local Business") before any outcome is stated.

### Recommended positioning statement (one-liner)

> **Render builds websites that get Morris County businesses found, called, and booked, backed by two founders you can actually reach.**

This puts the customer's result first (found / called / booked), keeps the local anchor (Morris County), and demotes "founder-led" to a trailing proof clause instead of the headline.

### Messaging hierarchy

**Primary promise:** A website (and the automation behind it) that turns local search traffic into paying customers, live in weeks, at a price you see before you ever get on a call.

**Three supporting pillars:**

1. **Built to convert, not just built.** Modern, fast-loading sites engineered for local search and for turning a visitor into a call or booking, not a template that just sits there.
   - *Proof:* Live, clickable demo sites (Fuego Street Tacos, Hartline Builders, Marlowe Dental). Core Web Vitals performance work already stated in trust strip. Local SEO included standard.

2. **Priced in the open.** Every package has a published number on the page. No "request a quote" gate, no discovery call required just to learn what something costs.
   - *Proof:* Full pricing table live on `/services`, direct contrast with JSN Marketing's gated "free audit" funnel that shows no pricing anywhere.

3. **You talk to the people doing the work.** No account manager, no layers, no handoff between the person who sold you and the person who builds it.
   - *Proof:* Named founders (Tim, Luke), direct email and Calendly, both on every project.

This hierarchy also gives you the exact frame to beat JSN Marketing with, point for point: they hide pricing, you publish it; they have no named team, you have two; they have zero case studies, you have live clickable work. Section 5 of the brief (competitor snapshot) confirms all three gaps are real and current.

### Hero H1 + subhead options (outcome/pain-led)

Current: **"We Build the Web for Local Business."** Sub: "Render is a founder-led studio building fast, modern websites and practical AI tools for local businesses across Morris County, NJ." This is brand-forward (leads with "we," "studio," "founder-led") not outcome-forward, and "practical AI tools" is vague enough to mean nothing.

| Option | H1 | Subhead |
|---|---|---|
| A | **A Website That Actually Brings You Customers.** | Render builds fast, local-SEO-ready websites for Morris County businesses. Live in 2 to 4 weeks, with pricing you see before you ever book a call. |
| B | **More Calls. More Bookings. One New Website.** | We build modern, fast-loading sites for Morris County businesses that turn Google searches into paying customers, not just a nicer-looking brochure. |
| C | **Your Website Should Be Working As Hard As You Do.** | Render builds and launches fast, search-ready websites for Morris County businesses in 2 to 4 weeks. Founder-built, plainly priced, no account managers. |
| D | **Get Found. Get Called. Get Booked.** | Render builds websites and simple AI automation that do the follow-up work local businesses don't have time for. Morris County, NJ. |
| E | **Local Customers Are Searching Right Now. Is Your Site Showing Up?** | Render builds fast, local-SEO-ready websites for Morris County businesses, built and run by the two people you'll actually talk to. |

**Recommended: Option A.** It leads with the outcome (customers, not "the web"), is concrete and declarative rather than a gimmick question, and the subhead does three jobs in one breath: what you get (SEO-ready site), when (2 to 4 weeks), and the risk-reversal (see pricing before you book). It also sets up the "priced in the open" pillar immediately, which is Render's sharpest edge over JSN Marketing's gated funnel. Keep the footnote line as-is ("Fixed, published pricing. Clear scope. You see the number before you ever book a call.") since it reinforces the same proof and is already tight.

## 4. Value proposition rewrites: Web and AI

Current service copy leans on adjectives ("modern," "custom," "fast") instead of naming the business result. Rewritten to pass the copy-editing "So What" test at every line.

### Web Design & Development

**Current (WhatWeDo card):** "Custom Next.js sites that load fast, rank in local search, and turn visitors into customers."

**Current (Services H2 + body):** "Modern websites for businesses that mean business." / "We build and launch sites for local businesses across Morris County, NJ, with a process that gets you live in 2-4 weeks."

**Rewrite:**

> **H2: Websites built to get you more customers.**
> Most local business sites are slow, outdated, or invisible on Google. We build yours from scratch: fast to load, easy to find in local search, and built around one goal, turning a visitor into a call, a booking, or a form fill. Live in 2 to 4 weeks. You see the price before you talk to us.

Why this works: opens with the competitor problem (slow, outdated, invisible) before the solution, which is the "problem/pain" section the copywriting skill recommends above the fold on service pages. Every clause answers "so what" (fast to load *so a visitor doesn't leave*, easy to find *so they find you before a competitor*, one goal *so you know what you're paying for*).

### AI Consulting & Automation

**Current:** "Quiet automation for the work you shouldn't be doing by hand." Use cases listed but the body is a vague "about small reliable systems" per the brief summary, no concrete claim.

**Rewrite:**

> **H2: The follow-up work costing you customers, automated.**
> Every missed after-hours message, every lead that sits in an inbox for two days, every review request you meant to send but didn't, that's a customer choosing whoever answered first. We build small, reliable systems that catch it: after-hours questions triaged overnight, new leads routed straight to your phone or CRM, review requests that send themselves, SOPs that stay current without anyone remembering to update them.

Why this works: names the cost of inaction (a customer picks the competitor who answered first) before listing features, which triggers loss aversion (a real principle from the marketing-psychology skill: people work harder to avoid a loss than to chase an equivalent gain). The four use cases already on the site are good and concrete, they were just buried under vague framing. Kept them, cut the fog around them.

## 5. Pricing presentation

### Tier names, order, anchoring
Starter / Standard / Premium in ascending order with Standard highlighted is a textbook Good-Better-Best layout and the pricing skill's contrast principle is already working correctly: Premium at $2,000 makes Standard's $750 look reasonable, and the highlighted middle tier nudges the "default effect" toward the plan you want most people to pick. Keep the structure. Two fixes:

1. **Make the highlight say something, not just look different.** Right now "highlighted" is a visual treatment. Add an explicit label, "Most Chosen" or "Recommended," on the Standard card. This activates social proof (bandwagon effect) and the "Paradox of Choice" principle, tell people which box to pick and more people pick it, full stop.
2. **Name badge instead of tier rename.** Don't rename Starter/Standard/Premium. Plain names read as more credible to a skeptical SMB buyer than cute names like "Launch/Grow/Dominate," which read as agency-template. Keep the plain names, add the social-proof badge instead.

### The $500 low-price signal risk
This is real and worth addressing directly rather than hoping nobody notices. A $500 website next to a rebrand aiming for "extremely professional" creates a credibility gap: cheap can read as junior or unproven, especially against a nationwide competitor with louder (if less substantive) marketing. Three ways to fix it, pick one:

- **Best option: name the reason it's cheap.** Add a line directly on the pricing section: *"Founding client pricing. These are the rates for our first clients while we build out our portfolio. They go up once we do."* This reframes low price from "we don't know what we're worth" into scarcity and urgency (a real psychological lever, not a trick, since the brief confirms this is genuinely the plan: raise monthly tiers after 2 to 3 clients). It also creates a reason to book now instead of waiting.
- **Alternative: raise the Starter price now.** Move Starter to $750 to close the gap with market rate ($1,500 to $5,000 per the brief) and shift Standard/Premium up accordingly. Higher-friction but removes the signal risk permanently instead of narrating around it.
- **Do both eventually:** ship the "founding client pricing" framing now (zero-cost, immediate), then raise prices for real once you have 2 to 3 case studies to backstop the higher number with actual proof.

**Recommendation: ship the founding-client framing immediately.** It is honest (matches the actual plan in the brief), costs nothing to implement, and turns a weakness into urgency.

### Fourth tier: add a "Custom" tier
Yes, add one. Two reasons: it directly answers the brief's open question about only offering two headline services (a Custom/Scale tier is the natural place to mention AI automation, multi-location builds, or e-commerce without cluttering the three fixed tiers), and it caps the pricing table with a high, unpriced anchor that makes Premium's $2,000 look moderate by contrast (anchoring effect, same mechanic JSN Marketing's competitors use with "starting at" enterprise tiers, except Render can still stay transparent by keeping the first three numbers published).

> **Custom / Scale**: *Let's talk numbers.*
> Multiple locations, e-commerce, or AI automation built into the build. We'll scope it and give you a fixed number before you commit to anything.

This preserves the "no surprise quote calls" brand promise (you get a fixed number, just after a scoping conversation) while giving the sales team a category for anything bigger than Premium.

### Monthly plan framing
Currently the monthly figure ($50/$75/$125) sits cold next to the build price with no framing, and the FAQ (not the pricing card) is the only place that says it's optional. Fix: put "Optional" directly on each card next to the monthly number, and reframe the number using mental accounting, since $50/mo sounds like a bill but "under $2 a day" sounds like nothing. Also consider an annual prepay option (pricing skill's standard 15 to 20 percent annual discount) once monthly plans exist long enough to model churn, this is a P2, not needed for the current pass.

### Rewritten pricing card copy

**Starter, $500 + $50/mo**
> **Starter**
> For a business that needs a clean, professional site online fast.
> $500 to build, $50/mo optional (about $1.65/day) to keep it updated and running.
> 1 to 3 pages, mobile-ready, contact form, basic on-page SEO, Google Analytics set up from day one.
> *Founding client pricing, limited to our first clients.*

**Standard, $750 + $75/mo, "Most Chosen"**
> **Standard**: Most Chosen
> For a business ready to show up in local search and look like the obvious choice.
> $750 to build, $75/mo optional (about $2.50/day).
> 4 to 6 pages, service-specific layouts, blog or news section, local SEO setup, performance tuning, analytics and conversion tracking so you know what's working.
> *Founding client pricing, limited to our first clients.*

**Premium, $2,000 + $125/mo**
> **Premium**
> For a business that wants bookings and leads running on autopilot.
> $2,000 to build, $125/mo optional (about $4.10/day).
> 7+ pages, booking or lead-capture workflow built in, custom animation and interactive sections, expanded local SEO, a full analytics dashboard, and priority launch scheduling.
> *Founding client pricing, limited to our first clients.*

**Custom / Scale**
> **Custom**
> Multiple locations, online ordering, or AI automation built into the build.
> We'll scope it with you and hand you a fixed number, never a vague "contact us for a quote."

## 6. Objection-handling copy

The six strongest objections a Morris County SMB owner will actually have, based on the brief's known gaps, the pricing pass above, and standard SMB buying friction. Two of these (design control, timeline risk) have **no current FAQ answer at all**, that's a real gap, not just weak copy.

| # | Objection (in the owner's own words) | Reassurance line / FAQ answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "$500 for a website feels cheap. What's the catch?" | *"It's founding client pricing. We're a new studio and these are our first-client rates, they go up once we've got a full portfolio. You get an agency-quality build at less than half the market rate, and every site we ship is our best sales pitch, so we're not cutting corners to hit a number."* |
| 2 | "You're students. Will you still be around, and do you actually know what you're doing?" | *"Tim builds the sites and the AI systems, he's a Computer Science student at Virginia Tech who's shipped real, live client and demo work you can click through right now. Luke runs strategy. We're both named, both reachable by email and phone, and both on every project start to finish, no handoff to a junior you've never talked to."* (Note: this answer leads with what he builds, not the research symposium placement. See section 4.) |
| 3 | "I get all my customers from Facebook and word of mouth already. Why do I need a website?" | Existing FAQ answer is good, tighten it slightly: *"Those referrals still look you up before they call. What they find is whatever Google and Yelp say about you right now. A website is the one place online you fully control, it shows up when locals search for what you do, it works for the two-thirds of people who aren't on Facebook, and it never puts a competitor's ad next to your name."* |
| 4 | "What if I don't like how it looks? How many changes do I get?" (currently unanswered anywhere on the site) | New FAQ: *"You'll see the design before it's final, and revisions are part of the build, not an extra charge. We're not done until the site looks right to you, not just to us."* |

| 6 | "How do I know 2 to 4 weeks is real and my launch won't drag for months?" (currently unanswered anywhere on the site) | New FAQ: *"2 to 4 weeks starts once we have your content and direction, that's the one variable in your control. We'll tell you exactly what we're waiting on at every step, so 'still in progress' never turns into radio silence."* |
