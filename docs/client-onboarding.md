# Client Onboarding

## Recommended Flow

The onboarding process should be simple, repeatable, and professional.

Suggested flow:

1. Lead enters through outreach, Calendly booking, referral, or direct contact.
2. Lead books a free consultation through the embedded Calendly scheduler.
3. L&T diagnoses the business need and confirms fit.
4. L&T sends a short project summary and price.
5. Client pays a deposit through Stripe.
6. Client completes the Google Forms intake form.
7. L&T reviews intake answers and requests missing assets.
8. Project starts.
9. Client reviews draft.
10. Revisions are completed.
11. Final payment is collected.
12. Site launches and handoff is completed.
13. Optional maintenance starts.

The public website should prioritize getting the lead onto the call. Detailed project information belongs in the post-call intake form.

## Intake Form

Create a structured intake form after the sales call. Do not rely on email threads for core project information.

Recommendation: start with Google Forms, not Typeform.

Why:

- It is free and already fits the Google Sheets and Google Drive workflow.
- Responses can flow directly into Google Sheets for later automation and lead analysis.
- It is easy for both founders to edit.
- It is good enough for post-call intake because the client has already shown interest.

Typeform can come later if the intake form becomes a public-facing conversion asset or if the experience needs to feel more polished.

This is separate from public booking. The Google Forms intake is for project details after the lead has been qualified.

The intake form should collect:

- Business name
- Owner/contact name
- Email and phone
- Current website URL if one exists
- Domain access details or who manages the domain
- Hosting details if relevant
- Logo files
- Brand colors if known
- Photos and videos
- Services offered
- Service areas
- Business hours
- Address
- Social links
- Google Business Profile link
- Main competitors
- Websites they like
- Websites they dislike
- Main goal of the website
- Required pages
- Contact form destination email
- Frequently asked customer questions
- Testimonials or reviews
- Any legal disclaimers or industry requirements

## If They Do Not Have a Website

If the client does not have a website already, the intake should focus on business fundamentals:

- What does the business sell?
- Who are the best customers?
- What areas do they serve?
- What makes them different?
- What should a visitor do next?
- What are the top 3-5 services?
- What proof do they have: reviews, photos, certifications, years in business?

For new businesses, L&T may need to help write more of the copy. That should be priced into the project.

## Timeline

Recommended default timeline:

- Intake and asset collection: 2-4 business days
- Website structure and content plan: 1-2 business days
- Website design/development: 5-10 business days
- Client review: 2-3 business days
- Revisions and launch prep: 2-5 business days
- Automation setup if included: 5-10 business days

Public promise:

```text
Most website projects launch in 2-4 weeks after we receive the needed content and access.
```

## Payment Structure

Recommended structure for early projects:

- 50% deposit to start
- 50% before launch
- Maintenance billed monthly if selected
- Minimum founding-client project floor: `$500`

For smaller projects, full payment upfront can also be reasonable.

For larger projects, use milestones:

- 40% deposit
- 30% after first draft
- 30% before launch

## Stripe Payment Links vs Website Checkout

Recommendation: start with Stripe Payment Links after the client has agreed to the project.

Why:

- It is professional enough.
- It is fast to set up.
- It avoids building a complex checkout system too early.
- It keeps pricing flexible while the offer is still changing.
- It lets L&T send a custom payment link that matches the agreed scope.

A website checkout can come later for fixed packages.

Best current approach:

- Website has "Book a Call" as the primary call to action, opening the embedded Calendly scheduler with no required lead form.
- After the call, send a proposal summary and Stripe deposit link.
- Eventually add package checkout for standardized services if the offer becomes repeatable.

## Project Start Criteria

Do not start production work until:

- Scope is clear.
- Price is agreed.
- Deposit is paid.
- Intake form is completed enough to begin.
- Required access or assets are available.

This protects the schedule and prevents unclear projects from taking over.

## Client Communication

Keep communication simple:

- Email for official project updates
- Call or video call for important decisions
- Shared form for intake
- Shared preview link for review

Future automation can organize this better, but the first version should be easy to run manually.

## Revisions

Unlimited revisions can be used as a strong early marketing promise, but it needs scope boundaries.

Recommended wording:

```text
Unlimited revisions before launch within the agreed project scope.
```

This means:

- Clients can request changes until the site feels right.
- L&T is not charging for every small adjustment.
- The client cannot turn a 5-page site into a 15-page site without a scope change.
- Brand-new features, major page additions, and changed business requirements can still be quoted separately.

This keeps the promise generous without making the project impossible to control.

## Maintenance

Maintenance should be optional for now.

Founding-client maintenance can include:

- Hosting coordination or hosting coverage if L&T chooses to manage it
- Small website edits
- Basic monthly performance or traffic report
- On-site SEO tweaks
- Minor content updates
- Basic uptime and form checks

Start founding-client maintenance at `$50/mo`.

Keep the price low while the business is young, but define a monthly time boundary internally. A practical starting cap is 1-2 hours per month at this price, even if that cap is not emphasized heavily in marketing.

Anything beyond the maintenance bucket should be quoted separately:

- New pages
- Redesigns
- New automations
- Major SEO campaigns
- New integrations
- Frequent weekly content work

## Handoff

At launch, send:

- Live website URL
- Summary of what was built
- Basic editing or support instructions if relevant
- Maintenance option
- Final invoice or payment confirmation
- Request for testimonial if the client is happy
