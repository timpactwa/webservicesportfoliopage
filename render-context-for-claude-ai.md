## 1. The Business

**Render** is a local web design, on-site SEO, and AI automation business founded by **Tim Pactwa** (CS, Virginia Tech) and **Luke Wilkins** (Finance, Virginia Tech).

- **Market**: Morris County, NJ local small businesses (expansion to broader NJ / other local-service regions later)
- **Domain**: `renderlocalwebsites.com`
- **Contact**: `renderlocalwebsites@gmail.com`
- **Services**: web design/development (primary), AI consulting and automation (secondary), on-site SEO (bundled into every website build, not sold standalone yet)

### Positioning

Lead with the business outcome — better websites, better local visibility, less manual work — not with "student-run." The founder story (lean, direct, no agency overhead) is supporting proof, introduced after the visitor understands the offer, not the headline.

Core voice: clear, confident, honest, modern, direct, not corporate. Avoid generic-agency template language, overpromising AI outcomes, vague buzzwords, fake case studies, enterprise language.

### Target customer

- Local small business owners in Morris County, NJ with outdated websites or no real web presence
- Owners who want practical help, not a large agency retainer
- Good target categories: landscapers, contractors, salons/barbershops, restaurants/cafes, fitness studios, medical/wellness practices, dentists, real estate agents, small retailers, professional services
- Best early targets: visibly outdated/slow/broken-on-mobile sites, missing clear CTAs, or poor service explanations

### Pricing (current, public)

- Starter: $500 + $50/mo
- Standard: $750 + $75/mo
- Premium: $2,000 + $125/mo

This is priced deliberately low as a founding-client wedge — market rate for comparable local-service website builds is $1,500–5,000 with $95–195/mo care plans. Plan is to raise monthly tiers after landing 2–3 clients. Pricing is transparent and public (no "book a call to find out" gate) — this is treated as a core differentiator, not just a cost choice.

AI automation is *not* bundled into every website package by default — sold as an add-on ($250–750 founding-client price) or standalone proof-of-concept ($500+) only when there's a clear, narrow pain point. Good automation examples: contact form routing + follow-up email, lead intake → spreadsheet/CRM, review request sequences, inquiry triage, internal doc generation, simple reporting dashboards.

### Sales motion

1. Build lead list (currently via a private Lead Finder tool + local directories like Randolph Local)
2. Manually verify site quality/existence before outreach
3. Personalized email referencing a specific, real observation about their site
4. Follow up by phone 1–2 days later
5. Free consultation call (diagnostic, not just pitch) requested via the on-site form, time confirmed by email
6. If a fit: short project summary + Stripe deposit link + Google Forms intake → build starts
7. Delivery target: 2–4 weeks depending on scope/responsiveness

Sales research benchmarks in use: ~7% SMB cold-email reply rate; a 3-7-7 day follow-up cadence captures ~93% of eventual replies; cold outreach volume should warm up gradually (5–10/day for 4–6 weeks); best send windows Tue–Thu 8–11am; subject lines under 6 words.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

### JSN Marketing Solutions (jsn-marketing.com)

A nationwide competitor selling a bundled "systems" package (website + AI voice receptionist + paid ads/SEO + automated follow-up + CRM) to appointment-based local service businesses (contractors, home services, law firms, medical/dental, med spas, salons, real estate).

**Key facts**:
- Florida LLC (Parkland, FL), no disclosed founders, no team page, no LinkedIn presence
- Tagline: "Stop losing leads to outdated websites"
- Sells via a gated "free audit" — no pricing anywhere on the site, no visible phone/email, everything funnels to a contact form
- Claims coverage in all 50 states via ~200 templated city/state location pages (programmatic SEO, not real local presence)
- **Zero discoverable social proof**: no case studies, no testimonials, no client logos, no reviews found on Google/Yelp/BBB/Trustpilot/Clutch, no confirmed social media presence
- Content is templated near-verbatim across industry verticals (same 4-pain-point structure reused with word-swaps)
- No blog, no changelog, no visible iteration signal

**Their strengths vs. Render**: broader bundled service menu (AI voice agents, paid ads, CRM), louder nationwide scale claim.

**Render's edge vs. them**: named/credentialed founders vs. anonymous LLC; real local presence in Morris County vs. a templated NJ location page from a Florida company; transparent public pricing vs. total pricing opacity behind a sales-call gate; direct founder access vs. an unnamed team; a growing real portfolio vs. zero case studies anywhere on their site.

**Read on them**: this looks like more marketing surface than proven track record — no evidence of real traction. The competitive risk is narrative (a nationwide-sounding brand can look more credible at a glance than a real local one), not substance. Render's counter is making "real name, real place, real price" impossible to miss in the copy.
